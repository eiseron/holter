## Why this exists

A source license governs the use of code. It does not govern the use of brand. Trademark policy protects users by ensuring that the names "Eiseron" and "Holter" identify a specific organization and its products — not arbitrary forks that may have different quality, security, or behavior.

This separation between code license and trademark policy is standard practice for source-available and open source projects (see, for example, the trademark policies of PostgreSQL, Mozilla, and the Linux Foundation).
